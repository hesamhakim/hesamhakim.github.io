PhenoMotion

Animated summaries of published biology papers — the mechanism, the evidence, and what the authors actually showed.

What it is

PhenoMotion turns a single published paper into a short narrated video: a schematic of the mechanism the paper proposes, then each result drawn from the paper's own source data, with its sample size and its statistical test on screen beside it.

Every number shown is taken from the published figures or the source-data files that accompany them. Claims are kept to what the figure supports — if something was shown in a dish and not in an animal, the video says so.

How the videos are made

  • Charts and schematics are drawn programmatically with Manim Community Edition, from the paper's data.
  • Narration is a synthetic voice. No part of any video is a recording of a person, and each video is disclosed to YouTube as containing synthetic media.
  • Each video names the paper, its DOI and its licence, and links to the original. These summaries are not the work of the papers' authors, and any error in a summary is the summariser's, not theirs.

The uploader

This site also serves as the home page for the small program that publishes these videos to YouTube. It runs on one researcher's own computer, signs in as that same person, and uploads their own videos to their own channel. It has no other users and collects nothing.
